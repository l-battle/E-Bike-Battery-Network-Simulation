Urban Battery Infrastructure Digital Twin for E-Bike Swap Networks
Project Overview
The rapid growth of urban e-bike delivery services has created increasing demand for scalable battery swap infrastructure. Companies such as Powershelter provide physical battery locker networks that allow delivery riders and e-bike users to exchange depleted batteries for charged ones throughout a city.
However, optimizing such infrastructure is difficult in real-world environments due to operational complexity, fluctuating demand, weather conditions, rider behavior, and the high cost of live experimentation. Testing new locker placements, redistribution strategies, or charging policies directly in the field can disrupt operations and requires substantial resources.
This project proposes the development of a digital twin simulation platform for urban e-bike battery swap infrastructure. The platform will simulate riders, lockers, battery inventory, charging cycles, and urban demand patterns within a virtual city environment. The goal is to create a system capable of modeling and evaluating infrastructure performance under different operational scenarios.
In addition, the platform will incorporate AI-assisted forecasting and decision-support features to predict demand surges, identify potential battery shortages, and evaluate redistribution strategies.
Problem Statement
Current battery swap systems primarily focus on rider-facing applications such as navigation, battery level monitoring, and swap availability. However, there is limited focus on infrastructure-level simulation and operational planning tools that allow providers to safely test and optimize their systems before implementing costly real-world changes.
Key operational questions include:
Where should new lockers be placed?
Which lockers are likely to experience shortages?
How do weather conditions affect battery demand?
How should charged batteries be redistributed across the network?
How does the system behave under increased rider demand?
Real-world experimentation is expensive, time-consuming, and operationally risky. A simulation environment could allow infrastructure providers to evaluate strategies virtually before deployment.
Proposed Solution
The proposed system will function as a digital simulation and decision-support platform for e-bike battery infrastructure.
The platform will:
Simulate urban rider traffic and battery swap behavior
Model locker inventory and charging availability
Incorporate environmental variables such as weather and time-of-day demand
Visualize battery flows and shortage events
Forecast demand using machine learning models
Generate operational recommendations for redistribution and infrastructure planning
The project is not intended to replicate existing rider-facing battery applications. Instead, it focuses on infrastructure-level operational intelligence and simulation.

Feasibility and Constraints
Due to limited access to proprietary operational data and hardware telemetry, the initial system will rely heavily on:
synthetic/simulated data
simplified battery abstractions
public environmental data sources
If available, anonymized operational data from Powershelter may be incorporated to improve realism.
The project does not aim to develop advanced battery chemistry models or embedded hardware systems. Instead, the focus is on systems-level infrastructure simulation and operational decision support.
